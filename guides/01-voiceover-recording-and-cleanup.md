# Guide 1 — Recording & Cleaning Voiceover

You already edit fast. What's new is that your **voice carries the whole video** now — there's no show footage to lean on. Viewers forgive cheap graphics. They don't forgive bad audio.

Work through this one step at a time. Each step ends with a check.

---

## Step 1 — Pick your mic setup (5 min)

Use what you already have, in this order of preference:
1. A USB mic (any brand) plugged into your computer.
2. Wired earbuds with a mic, clipped/held about a fist's width from your mouth.
3. Your phone's voice memo app, phone held 15–20 cm from your mouth, slightly off to the side.

Don't buy anything yet. Record the first 3 videos with what you've got and upgrade only if the test in Step 3 fails.

✅ **Check:** you know which device you're using.

## Step 2 — Fix the room, not the audio (10 min)

Echo is the #1 thing that makes faceless channels sound amateur, and software can't fully remove it.

- Record in the smallest, softest room you have (a closet full of clothes is the classic trick; a bedroom works).
- Put something soft behind your mic, facing you: a pillow, a folded blanket, a couch cushion.
- Turn off fans, AC, fridge-adjacent spots, notifications.
- Sit, don't stand — you'll stay a consistent distance from the mic.

✅ **Check:** clap once. If you hear a ring after the clap, add more soft stuff or change rooms.

## Step 3 — 30-second test recording

Read this out loud, in your normal fan-to-fan voice:

> "Alright, Season One, Episode Three. Ricky, Bubbles, a porno tape, and Mr. Lahey. Let's talk about it."

Play it back **on your phone speaker** and **on earbuds**. That's how most of your audience hears you.

✅ **Check:** no echo, no hiss you can hear without turning it up, and you don't sound like you're in a bathroom. If it fails, go back to Step 2 before buying anything.

## Step 4 — How to actually perform it

This is the part nobody tells you. A script read flat sounds like AI. You want it to sound like you're telling a buddy about the episode.

- **Read it twice silently first**, then once out loud as a warm-up you throw away.
- **Smile on the funny lines.** It's audible.
- **Record in short chunks** — one paragraph, or for Shorts, the whole thing 3 times in a row. Pick the best take later.
- **Flubbed a line? Don't stop.** Pause 2 seconds, clap once (it makes a spike you can see in the waveform), and re-read the line from the start of the sentence. You'll cut the bad one out in editing.
- **Quotes from the show:** say them in your own voice with a little attitude — don't do full impressions of the actors. Quick, a little bit of flavor, move on.
- **Energy:** Shorts = 10–15% faster and punchier than normal talking. Long-form = your normal speed, with pauses before big reveals (like the #1 episode).
- **Water, not coffee**, right before recording. Coffee = mouth clicks.

✅ **Check:** listen back. If any line sounds "read," re-record just that line.

## Step 5 — Where to record

**Option A (easiest): record straight into CapCut.** Use the microphone/Record button above the timeline. Good for Shorts.

**Option B (better for long-form): record on your phone or a free recorder app, then import the file.** You get a separate file you can re-use if a project ever breaks. Name every file the same way:

`S01E03_VO_take1.m4a` / `S01_RANKING_VO_part2.m4a`

✅ **Check:** your VO file is in the project folder with the right name.

## Step 6 — Clean it up in CapCut (the order matters)

Select your voiceover clip, open the **Audio** panel on the right, and go top to bottom:

1. **Cut the mistakes first.** Find your clap spikes in the waveform, cut out the bad takes, close the gaps.
2. **Tighten the gaps.** Trim pauses down to about a quarter-second between sentences. Long-form can breathe more (half a second) at section changes. This alone makes you sound 30% more confident.
3. **Reduce noise** → turn it on. If your voice starts sounding underwater/robotic, your room is too noisy — fix the room next time rather than leaning harder on the filter.
4. **Enhance voice** (if your version has it) → try it on, listen, keep it only if it sounds better on phone speakers. It can make some voices sound metallic.
5. **Loudness normalization** → turn it on. YouTube plays everything at roughly the same volume, so you want your voice consistently loud. If CapCut asks for a target, use around **-14 LUFS**.
6. **Volume of music under your voice:** music should sit way under you — start around **-20 to -25 dB below** your voice (in CapCut terms, drag the music clip volume down until you can barely notice it while you talk, then nudge it down a bit more).

(CapCut moves these menus around between versions. If you can't find one, tell me what you see in the Audio panel and I'll point you to it.)

✅ **Check:** play the finished section on your phone at half volume. Every word should be clear, and the music shouldn't fight you.

## Step 7 — Bleeps and swearing

This show swears constantly, and so will the quotes. For monetization:
- **Avoid strong swears in the first ~7 seconds** of any video (that's where YouTube is strictest about ad suitability).
- After that, mild/moderate swearing is generally OK, but **bleep or mute the strongest words in titles, thumbnails, and on-screen text**, and keep your own narration mostly clean — let the quote cards carry the flavor, with the word partly masked (`f**k`).
- Policies shift; if a video gets a yellow icon, tell me what's in the first 30 seconds and we'll adjust.

✅ **Check:** first 7 seconds are clean.
